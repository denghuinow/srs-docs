# Software Requirements Specification (SRS)
## Tarrant County Indigent Defense Attorney Management System (IDAMS)
**Version:** 1.0
**Date:** October 26, 2023
**Status:** Draft for Review

---

### 1. Introduction

#### 1.1 Purpose
This document defines the functional and non-functional requirements for the Tarrant County Indigent Defense Attorney Management System (IDAMS). The intended audience includes project stakeholders, system architects, software developers, quality assurance teams, and end-users from the Tarrant County criminal justice community. This SRS serves as the definitive specification for system development, testing, and acceptance.

#### 1.2 Scope
The IDAMS is a specialized software component of the county's Integrated Justice Information System (IJIS). Its core purpose is to automate and manage the end-to-end administrative workflow for appointing and compensating court-appointed defense attorneys for indigent defendants within Tarrant County's criminal justice process.

**In-Scope:**
*   Indigency determination request submission and processing.
*   Management of qualified attorney rosters and appointment selection via defined rotation methods.
*   Automated notification to defendants, appointed attorneys, and detention facilities.
*   Tracking of mandatory attorney-client contact.
*   Management of attorney compensation claims, including submission, judicial approval, and payment processing.
*   Scheduling of court settings and recording associated outcomes relevant to indigent defense administration.
*   Generation of statutory compliance reports for state oversight bodies.

**Out-of-Scope:**
*   Management of the underlying criminal court case details, evidence, or docketing (handled by the Court Case Management System).
*   Management of defense representation for non-indigent defendants.
*   Prosecutorial or District Attorney workflow management (handled by the ECFS).
*   General county financial management or payroll outside of attorney fee payment processing.

### 2. Overall Description

#### 2.1 Product Perspective
The IDAMS is a subsystem of the larger Tarrant County IJIS. It operates within a tightly integrated ecosystem of criminal justice information systems. It does not function as a standalone application but as a critical link between the point of arrest/detention, the courts, the defense bar, and county financial operations.

**System Interfaces:**
*   **Court Case Management System (CCMS):** Primary source for defendant/case data (e.g., cause number, charges, custody status). Receives updates on appointments and settings.
*   **District Attorney's ECFS:** Receives notification of attorney appointments and case filing decisions.
*   **External Accounting/Financial System:** Transmits approved compensation claims for payment.
*   **Email/Fax Servers:** Used for all external user notifications (attorneys, detention).

#### 2.2 Product Functions
The high-level functions of IDAMS are:
1.  **Request & Indigency Management:** Capture, process, and track requests for appointed counsel and indigency determinations.
2.  **Attorney & List Management:** Maintain attorney profiles, qualifications, and certified appointment lists. Execute rotation-based or judge-directed appointments.
3.  **Notification & Workflow Engine:** Automatically generate and send legally required notifications to all parties based on system events.
4.  **Contact & Activity Tracking:** Record and monitor defense attorney interactions with their clients as mandated.
5.  **Compensation Management:** Facilitate the submission, review, approval, and payment processing of attorney fee vouchers and expenses.
6.  **Court Setting Integration:** Schedule settings and record dispositions relevant to tracking the appointment lifecycle.
7.  **Reporting & Compliance:** Generate standardized and ad-hoc reports for operational and state compliance purposes (TFID).

#### 2.3 User Characteristics
| User Class | Skill Level | Primary Responsibilities |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Magistrate** | Proficient with basic judicial software. | Determine indigency at magistration; initiate request for appointed counsel in IDAMS. |
| **OAA Administrator** | Expert user, trained on system administration. | Process requests, manage attorney lists, run appointment rotations, troubleshoot workflow, generate reports. |
| **Judge** | Proficient with judicial and administrative software. | Approve compensation claims; may directly appoint attorneys from list; manage list membership for their court. |
| **Defense Attorney** | Computer-literate; variable proficiency. | Receive notifications; log client contact activities; submit compensation claims and expenses electronically. |
| **Detention Officer** | Basic computer skills. | Access system to view appointment notifications for inmates. |

#### 2.4 Constraints
*   **Legal & Regulatory:** System design and workflow are rigidly constrained by the Texas Code of Criminal Procedure, Texas Government Code, and the county's adopted Indigent Defense Plans.
*   **Integration Dependencies:** Successful operation is dependent on stable, bidirectional interfaces with the external CCMS and DA's ECFS.
*   **Data Standard:** Data exchange with external systems must comply with the Global Justice XML Data Model (GJXDM).
*   **Technical:** Must adhere to Texas DIR standards for electronic record-keeping, audit trails, and digital signatures where applicable.

#### 2.5 Assumptions and Dependencies
*   It is assumed that magistrates will have immediate access to the system at the point of magistration.
*   It is assumed that all appointed attorneys will have and use an email address for official notifications.
*   The system is dependent on the continuous availability and accurate data from the external CCMS for core defendant and case information.
*   The project timeline is dependent on the cooperation of external system custodians (Courts, DA) for interface development and testing.

### 3. Specific Requirements

#### 3.1 External Interface Requirements
**3.1.1 Software Interfaces (SI)**
*   **SI-1: Court Case Management System Interface**
    *   **Method:** Web service/API following GJXDM schema.
    *   **Input (Receive):** Defendant demographic data, booking data, cause number, filed charges, custody status, court assignments.
    *   **Output (Send):** Appointed attorney details, date/time of appointment, scheduled court settings.
*   **SI-2: District Attorney ECFS Interface**
    *   **Method:** Secure file transfer or web service.
    *   **Output (Send):** Notification of attorney appointment (including attorney details) and notification of case filing decision (e.g., complaint filed, declined).
*   **SI-3: Financial System Interface**
    *   **Method:** Batch file transfer (e.g., XML, CSV).
    *   **Output (Send):** Approved payment vouchers with payee ID, amount, case reference, and approval authority.

**3.1.2 Communications Interfaces (CI)**
*   **CI-1: Email Notification Interface**
    *   **Protocol:** SMTP.
    *   **Function:** Send templated, non-modifiable notifications to attorneys, judges, and OAA staff for events (appointment, claim status, hearing schedule).
*   **CI-2: Fax Notification Interface**
    *   **Protocol:** Integrated fax server API.
    *   **Function:** Send official appointment notifications to detention facilities and as a fallback for attorneys without email.

#### 3.2 Functional Requirements
**3.2.1 Indigency Determination & Request Management (IDR)**
*   **IDR-1:** The system shall allow a Magistrate to search for a defendant via integration with CCMS.
*   **IDR-2:** The system shall provide a form for the Magistrate to record the indigency determination (Indigent / Not Indigent).
*   **IDR-3:** Upon a finding of indigence, the system shall automatically create a "Request for Appointed Counsel" linked to the defendant and case.
*   **IDR-4:** The system shall timestamp the request and assign a status of "Pending Assignment."

**3.2.2 Attorney List & Appointment Management (AAM)**
*   **AAM-1:** The system shall maintain appointment lists (e.g., Felony, Misdemeanor, Appellate) with qualified attorneys.
*   **AAM-2:** OAA Administrators shall be able to add/remove attorneys from lists, subject to judicial approval for specific courts.
*   **AAM-3:** For a pending request, the system shall, upon OAA action, select the next eligible attorney from the appropriate list using a configurable rotation algorithm (e.g., straight rotation, weighted by prior appointments).
*   **AAM-4:** The system shall allow a Judge to override the rotation and select a specific attorney from the list.
*   **AAM-5:** Upon appointment, the system shall record the attorney of record and timestamp the assignment.

**3.2.3 Notification Management (NOT)**
*   **NOT-1:** Within 24 hours of appointment, the system shall automatically generate and send notifications to:
    *   The appointed attorney (via email).
    *   The detention facility where the defendant is held (via fax/email).
    *   The District Attorney's office (via ECFS interface).
*   **NOT-2:** Notifications shall contain specific, non-editable data: Defendant name, cause number, charges, appointed attorney name/contact, appointment date.

**3.2.4 Attorney Activity Tracking (ACT)**
*   **ACT-1:** The system shall provide a secure portal for Defense Attorneys to log contacts with their client (date, time, method [in-person, phone], summary).
*   **ACT-2:** The system shall alert OAA if mandatory initial contact (e.g., within 3 business days) is not logged.

**3.2.5 Compensation Management (COM)**
*   **COM-1:** The system shall provide a form for Defense Attorneys to submit fee vouchers and expense claims, linked to the appointed case.
*   **COM-2:** Submitted claims shall route electronically to the presiding Judge for review and approval.
*   **COM-3:** Approved claims shall be marked "Ready for Payment" and their data formatted for export to the financial system.
*   **COM-4:** The system shall track the status of all claims (Submitted, Under Review, Approved, Denied, Paid).

**3.2.6 Reporting (REP)**
*   **REP-1:** The system shall generate the standard TFID Annual Report, including statistics on:
    *   Number of appointments per attorney/court/case type.
    *   Timeliness of appointments relative to request.
    *   Average and total costs of indigent defense.
    *   Case dispositions.
*   **REP-2:** The system shall allow OAA users to generate ad-hoc reports based on date ranges, courts, attorneys, and case types.

#### 3.3 Non-Functional Requirements
**3.3.1 Legal & Compliance (LC)**
*   **LC-1:** The system shall enforce business rules derived from Texas Code of Criminal Procedure Articles 26.04 and 26.05 regarding appointment timelines and compensation caps.
*   **LC-2:** All electronic records and signatures shall be managed in accordance with the Texas UETA and DIR guidelines, providing a secure audit trail.

**3.3.2 Performance (PER)**
*   **PER-1:** The system shall process a request for appointment and initiate notifications within 4 business hours of magistrate submission 99% of the time.
*   **PER-2:** System search operations (for defendant, attorney, case) shall return results in less than 3 seconds under normal load.

**3.3.3 Security (SEC)**
*   **SEC-1:** Access shall be role-based (RBAC), with permissions defined per user class (Magistrate, Judge, Attorney, OAA Admin).
*   **SEC-2:** All PHI/PII data shall be encrypted at rest and in transit.
*   **SEC-3:** A comprehensive audit log shall record all creations, modifications, and views of key data (determinations, appointments, payments).

**3.3.4 Reliability & Availability (REL)**
*   **REL-1:** The system shall be available 99.5% during core business hours (6:00 AM - 8:00 PM CT, Monday-Friday).
*   **REL-2:** The system shall have a disaster recovery plan allowing restoration of service within 8 hours of a major outage.

**3.3.5 Integration (INT)**
*   **INT-1:** Data exchange with all external systems shall conform to the GJXDM standard.
*   **INT-2:** The system shall be designed to handle temporary unavailability of external interfaces (CCMS, ECFS) by queueing outbound messages and providing administrative alerts.

### 4. Supporting Information

#### 4.1 Priority & Phasing
**Priority 1 (Critical for Legal Compliance & Launch):**
*   Indigency Request submission (IDR-1 to IDR-4).
*   Basic rotation-based appointment (AAM-1, AAM-3, AAM-5).
*   Mandatory 24-hour notifications (NOT-1).
*   Core TFID reporting (REP-1).
*   Integration with CCMS for defendant/case data (SI-1).

**Priority 2 (Essential for Operational Efficiency):**
*   Attorney portal for contact logging and claim submission (ACT-1, COM-1).
*   Judicial workflow for claim approval (COM-2, COM-3).
*   Advanced list management and judicial override (AAM-2, AAM-4).
*   Interface with Financial System (SI-3).

**Priority 3 (Enhancements):**
*   Advanced alerting and dashboard features.
*   Sophisticated ad-hoc reporting builder.

#### 4.2 Acceptance Criteria
Formal acceptance of the IDAMS will require:
1.  Successful execution of test cases validating all Priority 1 and 2 functional requirements.
2.  Verification of all non-functional requirements, particularly performance benchmarks (PER-1) and security controls (SEC-1, SEC-2).
3.  A successful integration test event demonstrating end-to-end data flow with the live or staging environments of the external Court Case Management System and the District Attorney's ECFS.
4.  Certification by the County Legal and Compliance office that the system's workflows adhere to specified Texas statutes and the county's Indigent Defense Plan.
