**Purpose & Scope**
The system manages the appointment and administration of court-appointed defense attorneys for indigent defendants in Tarrant County's criminal justice process. It handles requests, attorney selection, notifications, compensation, and compliance reporting. It does not manage the underlying court cases or non-indigent defense.

**Product Background / Positioning**
This is a component of the county's Integrated Justice Information System (IJIS), intended to streamline information sharing and administrative efficiency across the criminal justice community. It must integrate with external systems like the Court Case Management System and the District Attorney's Electronic Case Filing System.

**Core Functional Overview**
*   Submit and process requests for appointed counsel, including indigency determination.
*   Manage attorney appointment lists and selection rotations.
*   Notify relevant parties (defendant, attorney, detention) of appointments and case filing decisions.
*   Track and manage defense attorney contact with defendants.
*   Manage the submission, approval, and payment of defense attorney compensation.
*   Schedule court settings and record their outcomes.
*   Generate comprehensive compliance reports for the Task Force on Indigent Defense.

**Key Users & Usage Scenarios**
Primary users are the Office of Attorney Appointments (central administrators), Magistrates, Judges, Defense Attorneys, and Detention Facility Officers. Magistrates determine indigency and submit requests. The Office processes requests, makes appointments, and manages lists. Defense Attorneys report contacts and submit payment claims. Judges approve payments and manage attorney lists.

**Major External Interfaces**
The system must interface with the external Court Case Management System, the District Attorney's Electronic Case Filing System (ECFS), and external accounting/financial systems. Interfaces with users are via electronic notifications (e.g., email/fax).

**Key Non-functional Requirements**
*   Legal Compliance: Must adhere to Texas Code of Criminal Procedure (specified articles), Texas Government Code, and the Texas Uniform Electronic Transactions Act.
*   Data Management: Must follow Texas Department of Information Resources guidelines for managing electronic transactions and signed records.
*   Integration: Must exchange data with specified external justice and financial systems.
*   Reporting: Must produce a defined set of statistical reports (appointments, timelines, costs, dispositions) for compliance monitoring.

**Constraints, Assumptions & Dependencies**
*   The system is constrained by Texas state law and the county's adopted Indigent Defense Plans.
*   It is dependent on integration with the existing Court Case Management System and the District Attorney's ECFS for data and process continuity.
*   It assumes the use of the Global Justice XML Data Model (GJXDM) for data exchange.

**Priorities & Acceptance Approach**
The highest priority is enabling compliance with legal procedural timelines (e.g., processing requests by the next working day, notifying within 24 hours). Core appointment management and reporting for the Texas Fair Defense Act are fundamental. Acceptance will be based on fulfilling the specified use cases, meeting legal and technical requirements, and successful integration with the mandated external systems.
