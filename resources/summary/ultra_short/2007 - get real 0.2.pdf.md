**Purpose & Scope**
The system is the Get Real website, intended to attract and encourage Oregon high school students to pursue a computer science degree within the Oregon University System. It provides career and educational information but does not directly handle student applications or university admissions.

**Product Background / Positioning**
This is version 2, an update to an existing website. It operates as part of the OUS domain (getreal.ous.edu) on OSU servers to maximize search engine visibility and leverage existing infrastructure.

**Core Functional Overview**
*   Provide listings and comparisons of computer science programs at Oregon universities.
*   Present career information, including job roles and salary data.
*   Host profiles ("Real People") of CS professionals and recent graduates.
*   Maintain a dedicated section with resources and profiles for women in computer science.
*   Offer a search function for content both internal and external to the site.
*   Display RSS feeds of current CS-related information.
*   Provide an FAQ section for questions answered by graduates or professors.

**Key Users & Usage Scenarios**
The primary users are Oregon high school students (grades 9-12). Two key groups are students already interested in CS seeking detailed information, and college-bound students not initially interested in CS who might be persuaded. A third group is students in other fields who might consider a CS minor.

**Major External Interfaces**
The website must be compatible with and tested against Internet Explorer, Firefox, and Netscape browsers. It interfaces with the broader OUS website domain and infrastructure.

**Key Non-functional Requirements**
*   Performance: Server response time must be comparable to the main OUS website to accommodate short student attention spans.
*   Maintainability: The site must adhere to the CSS and style conventions established in version 1 and OUS standards.
*   Usability: Design must avoid "walls of text," use visual elements, and facilitate fast page reading. Content and navigation must be adaptable based on the evaluation of site hits and user time spent.

**Constraints, Assumptions & Dependencies**
Constraints include limited server bandwidth for video streaming and limited availability of Chancellor’s Office development resources. The site is dependent on OUS servers and domain hosting.

**Priorities & Acceptance Approach**
Maintaining a "teen friendly" site with easy navigation and visually attractive design is of highest importance to hold attention. Acceptance is based on the site being hosted on an adequate server and its design being consistent with OUS conventions. Success will be evaluated by tracking site hits and time spent by users.
