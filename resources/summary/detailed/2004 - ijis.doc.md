# Detailed Summary: Manage Indigent Defense Services for Tarrant County IJIS

## Background and Scope
This specification defines the business requirements for the Manage Indigent Defense Services component of the Tarrant County Integrated Justice Information System (IJIS). The system aims to streamline the appointment, management, and compensation of court-appointed attorneys for indigent defendants, ensuring compliance with the Texas Fair Defense Act and related statutes. It focuses on automating processes from the initial request for counsel through case disposition and attorney payment. Non-goals include implementing full case management (handled by external systems) and managing non-indigent defense services.

## Stakeholders Matrix and Use Cases
*   **Defendant**: An arrested/charged individual who may request a court-appointed attorney if deemed indigent.
*   **Magistrate**: Informs the defendant of their rights, determines indigency, and submits the request for appointed counsel.
*   **Detention Facility Officer**: Captures defendant information and schedules the indigency hearing.
*   **Office of Attorney Appointments (OAA)**: Central office responsible for processing appointment requests, managing attorney lists, and coordinating notifications.
*   **Defense Attorney**: Attorney appointed to represent the defendant; submits contact reports and payment requests.
*   **District Attorney**: Represents the state; notifies the OAA of case filing decisions (accept/reject/modify charges).
*   **Judge (County/District)**: Presides over cases; approves attorney payment requests and attorney list applications.
*   **Court Coordinator**: Manages court schedules, enters setting information, and records hearing results.
*   **Auditor**: Manages county finances; processes approved payments to defense attorneys.
*   **Commissioners Court**: County governing body that approves claims for payment.
*   **Local Selection Committee**: Qualifies attorneys for appointment in capital felony cases where the death penalty is sought.
*   **Presiding Administrative Judge**: Reviews appeals of disapproved attorney payment requests.
*   **Task Force on Indigent Defense (TFID)**: State body that sets standards and receives compliance reports.
*   **Filing Agency**: Law Enforcement Agency that investigates and presents charges; may drop charges.
*   **Court Case Management System (CCMS)**: External system used by the justice community to manage case information.

**Main/Exception Scenarios (≤8 Total):**
1.  **Submit Request for Appointed Counsel (Basic)**: Defendant requests counsel, magistrate determines indigency, and submits request to OAA.
2.  **Submit Request (Exception: Waiver)**: Defendant waives right to appointed counsel.
3.  **Process Request for Appointed Counsel (Basic)**: OAA receives request, confirms defendant in custody, selects next attorney from rotation list, and notifies parties.
4.  **Process Request (Exception: Capital Case)**: For death penalty cases, the request is forwarded to a District Court Judge for selection of qualified attorneys.
5.  **Manage Defendant Contact (Basic)**: Appointed attorney contacts defendant and notifies OAA of contact dates.
6.  **Manage Defense Attorney Compensation (Basic)**: Attorney submits payment request, judge approves, Auditor processes payment.
7.  **Manage Attorney List (Basic)**: Attorney applies to be on rotation list, judges approve, OAA adds attorney to list.
8.  **Manage Reporting (Basic)**: OAA generates and distributes compliance reports to the TFID.

## Business Process
**Main Process: Appoint and Manage Defense Counsel (≤8 Steps)**
1.  **Trigger**: Defendant arrested and transported to detention.
    *   **Input**: Defendant, incident, and financial information.
    *   **Output**: Scheduled indigency hearing.
2.  Magistrate conducts hearing, determines indigency, and submits Request for Appointed Counsel to OAA.
    *   **Output**: Signed Request or Waiver.
3.  OAA processes request: confirms defendant in custody, checks for existing attorney, selects attorney from appropriate rotation list.
    *   **Output**: Attorney appointment notification.
4.  Appointed attorney contacts defendant and reports contact to OAA.
    *   **Output**: Contact notification.
5.  District Attorney notifies OAA of case filing decision (charges accepted/rejected/modified).
    *   **Output**: Case filing notice.
6.  Court Coordinator schedules settings; OAA notifies parties; results are recorded.
    *   **Output**: Setting notifications and results.
7.  Upon case activity or conclusion, defense attorney submits request for payment; judge reviews/approves; Auditor processes payment.
    *   **Output**: Approved payment to attorney.
8.  OAA compiles data and generates mandated reports for the TFID.
    *   **Output**: Compliance reports.

**Key Branch A: Defendant Not Indigent or Waives Counsel (≤4 Steps)**
1.  Magistrate determines defendant is not indigent OR defendant waives right to counsel.
2.  Magistrate submits notice of indigency determination or waiver to OAA.
3.  OAA records the denial/waiver.
4.  Process ends for appointment path.

**Key Branch B: Capital Felony Case (≤4 Steps)**
1.  OAA identifies request is for a capital felony case with death penalty sought.
2.  OAA forwards request to presiding District Court Judge.
3.  Judge selects two attorneys qualified by the Local Selection Committee.
4.  OAA appoints selected attorneys (outside standard rotation).

## Domain Model (Entities ≤8)
1.  **Defendant**: ID (required, unique), Personal Details, Financial Affidavit (required), Custody Status.
2.  **Case/Incident**: Case ID (required, unique), Charges, Filing Agency, Arrest Date/Time.
3.  **Request for Appointed Counsel**: Request ID (required, unique), Defendant (reference), Magistrate, Indigency Determination, Submission Date/Time.
4.  **Defense Attorney**: Attorney ID (required, unique), Contact Info, Qualifications, List Status (Active/Suspended/Removed).
5.  **Appointment**: Appointment ID (required, unique), Defendant (reference), Defense Attorney (reference), Case (reference), Appointment Date/Time.
6.  **Attorney Rotation List**: List ID (required, unique), Offense Type/Level, Ordered List of Attorneys.
7.  **Payment Request**: Request ID (required, unique), Defense Attorney (reference), Case (reference), Services Itemized, Amount, Status (Pending/Approved/Denied).
8.  **Court Setting**: Setting ID (required, unique), Case (reference), Date/Time, Type, Result.

## Interfaces and Integrations (≤8 Total)
1.  **System**: Court Case Management System (CCMS)
    *   **Direction**: Bidirectional
    *   **Interaction**: Data exchange for defendant/case info, appointment entry, and setting details.
    *   **Input Key**: Defendant ID, Case Number, Charge Details.
    *   **Output Key**: Appointment Record, Setting Information.
    *   **SLA**: Real-time or near-real-time synchronization for critical data (e.g., appointments).
2.  **System**: District Attorney Electronic Case Filing System (ECFS)
    *   **Direction**: Inbound
    *   **Interaction**: Notification of case filing decisions (accept/reject/modify charges).
    *   **Input Key**: Case Number, Filing Decision, Updated Charge List.
    *   **Output Key**: (N/A for inbound)
    *   **SLA**: Notification within 24 hours of decision.
3.  **System**: External Accounting System (e.g., SAP)
    *   **Direction**: Outbound
    *   **Interaction**: Forwarding approved payment orders for processing.
    *   **Input Key**: Approved Payment Request ID, Amount, Payee Info.
    *   **Output Key**: Payment Order.
    *   **SLA**: Batch transfer at end of business day.
4.  **System**: Email/Fax Notification Services
    *   **Direction**: Outbound
    *   **Interaction**: Sending notifications to defendants, attorneys, and officers.
    *   **Input Key**: Recipient Details, Notification Template, Appointment/Setting Data.
    *   **Output Key**: Sent notification receipt.
    *   **SLA**: Immediate delivery upon event trigger.

## Acceptance Criteria
**Capability: Submit and Process Request for Counsel**
*   **Given** a defendant is in custody and has an indigency hearing,
    **When** the magistrate submits a Request for Appointed Counsel,
    **Then** the OAA receives the request and can view the associated financial affidavit.
*   **Given** a valid request is received and the defendant is in custody,
    **When** the OAA processes the request,
    **Then** the next eligible attorney from the correct rotation list is selected, and notifications are sent to the defendant, attorney, and detention facility.

**Capability: Manage Attorney Payment**
*   **Given** a defense attorney has completed services,
    **When** they submit a detailed payment request through the system,
    **Then** the request is routed to the appropriate judge for review and approval.
*   **Given** a judge approves a payment request,
    **When** the OAA forwards the order to the Auditor,
    **Then** the payment is added to the queue for Commissioners Court approval and disbursement.

**Capability: Compliance Reporting**
*   **Given** the OAA needs to report to the TFID,
    **When** a user selects a report type and date range,
    **Then** the system generates a report containing the required metrics (e.g., average time to appointment, total payments by offense type).

## Non-Functional Metrics
*   **Performance**: 1) System must process and route a new Request for Appointed Counsel within 15 minutes of submission. 2) Standard reports for a quarterly date range must generate within 5 minutes.
*   **Reliability**: 1) System must maintain 99.5% uptime during court business hours (7 AM - 7 PM). 2) All electronic transactions and records must be backed up daily with a 30-day retention cycle.
*   **Security**: 1) Access to defendant financial affidavits and attorney payment details must be role-based and audited. 2) All electronic signatures and records must comply with Texas UETA and DIR guidelines.
*   **Compliance**: 1) All processes and data points must support adherence to Texas CCP Articles 1.051, 26.04, 26.05, and Government Code Section 71.0351. 2) System must enable reporting per the Tarrant County Indigent Defense Plan.
*   **Observability**: 1) All key state changes (request submitted, attorney appointed, payment approved) must be logged with timestamp and user. 2) System must provide dashboard views of pending requests and appointment turnaround times.

## Risk List and Mitigation Strategies (≤8 Items)
1.  **Risk**: Complex integration with legacy Court Case Management System causing delays.
    *   **Mitigation**: Early prototyping of integration points; involve CCMS technical team from the start.
2.  **Risk**: Strict legal and compliance requirements lead to overly complex or rigid software.
    *   **Mitigation**: Maintain traceability matrix linking each requirement to its legal source; prioritize configurability for rule-based logic.
3.  **Risk**: Resistance to change from judicial and administrative staff accustomed to paper-based processes.
    *   **Mitigation**: Develop comprehensive training programs and phased rollout with super-user support.
4.  **Risk**: Data quality issues from manually entered historical records affecting report accuracy.
    *   **Mitigation**: Implement data validation rules at entry; plan for a data cleansing phase for critical fields.
5.  **Risk**: Ensuring system availability during critical path activities (e.g., magistration hours).
    *   **Mitigation**: Design for high availability during business hours; establish clear manual fallback procedures.
6.  **Risk**: Managing electronic signatures and records to meet legal standards (UETA).
    *   **Mitigation**: Engage county legal counsel early to validate the technical approach for digital signatures and audit trails.
7.  **Risk**: Budget overruns due to scope creep in reporting requirements.
    *   **Mitigation**: Strict change control process for new report requests; deliver core reporting first, then enhancements.
8.  **Risk**: Security breaches exposing sensitive defendant financial or case information.
    *   **Mitigation**: Implement robust authentication, authorization, and encryption; conduct regular security audits.
