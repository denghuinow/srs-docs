# Short Summary: Outbreak Management Functional Requirements

## Background and Objectives
This document outlines the functional requirements for systems supporting the Public Health Information Network (PHIN) Outbreak Management area, which aims to aid in the investigation, monitoring, management, analysis, and reporting of health events or acts of bioterrorism. The primary goal is to collect and analyze data to support identifying, containing, and mitigating the effects of an outbreak.

## In Scope
*   Configurable system architecture to support new data fields, entities, and relationships for specific health events.
*   Data collection for various entities (persons, organizations, locations, animals, objects, conveyances, events, other organisms) and health event details.
*   Core functions including case investigation via electronic questionnaires, linking entities and data, and contact exposure tracing.
*   Analysis, visualization, and generation of reports, charts, maps, and aggregated data.
*   Secure, standards-based data exchange with other public health systems (e.g., laboratory, surveillance, countermeasure administration).

## Out of Scope
*   Detailed specifications for data transport mechanisms and message construction (referenced in separate cross-functional documents).
*   Comprehensive operational procedures for system backup, continuity, and training (referenced in separate cross-functional documents).
*   Detailed security, availability, and privacy implementation protocols (referenced in separate cross-functional documents).
*   Specific technical details for system deployment and configuration management.
*   In-depth processes for countermeasure administration and adverse event tracking (referenced in separate functional requirements).

## Stakeholders and Core Use Cases
*   **Investigators/Epidemiologists:** Public health professionals responsible for conducting case investigations, tracing contacts, and analyzing outbreak data.
*   **Laboratory Personnel:** Staff involved in processing specimen/sample test requests and reporting laboratory results.
*   **Public Health Administrators:** Managers who oversee outbreak response, monitor aggregate data, and generate reports for decision-making.
*   **System Configurators/IT Support:** Personnel who set up and configure the system, including field devices and agent-specific deployment packages.
*   **Response Partner Organizations:** External entities (e.g., other health departments, federal agencies) that exchange data during a multi-jurisdictional event.

**User Stories:**
1.  As an **Investigator**, I want to create and use electronic, configurable questionnaires so that I can efficiently collect standardized and event-specific case data.
2.  As an **Investigator**, I want to dynamically link cases, contacts, specimens, and laboratory results so that I can trace transmission pathways and identify at-risk populations.
3.  As a **Laboratory Personnel**, I want to receive electronic test requests and send back results that are automatically linked to the correct case so that diagnosis and reporting are accurate and timely.
4.  As a **Public Health Administrator**, I want to generate maps, epi-curves, and aggregate reports (e.g., case counts, vaccination totals) so that I can visualize the outbreak's scope and effectiveness of interventions.
5.  As a **System Configurator**, I want to define new entity types and data fields so that the system can be adapted to investigate unique or emerging health threats.
6.  As a **Response Partner Organization**, I want to securely exchange case, contact, and laboratory data with other jurisdictions so that we can coordinate a unified response to a cross-border outbreak.

## Success Metrics
*   Ability to electronically create and send laboratory test requests and receive/process laboratory results.
*   Ability to electronically exchange messages for confirmed/probable/suspect cases, investigations, exposure contacts, and administered countermeasures.
*   Effective containment of the health event as supported by system-enabled tracing, analysis, and reporting.

## Major Constraints
*   Systems must be configurable to meet the needs of different types of health events without requiring custom development for each event.
*   Data exchange must adhere to established vocabulary standards (e.g., LOINC, SNOMED) and message implementation guides.
*   Systems must support multiple deployment options (client-server, disconnected, web-based) and ensure data synchronization across instances.
*   All data collection and exchange must comply with privacy requirements to protect patient, organization, and personnel information.
*   Systems must be available and secure to ensure operational continuity during a public health emergency.

## Undecided Issues
*   Specific protocols for determining when follow-up and isolation/quarantine monitoring transitions from contact tracing to a focused countermeasure administration effort.
*   Detailed processes for ensuring unambiguous identification of animals involved in investigations.
*   Complete definition of all operational policies for communicating with external stakeholders (e.g., media, public).
*   Finalized grouping methodology for message components by observation type (e.g., laboratory, symptom).
*   Comprehensive list of all supported file formats for data import and export.
