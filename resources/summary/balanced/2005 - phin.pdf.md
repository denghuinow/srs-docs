# Balanced Summary: PHIN Outbreak Management Functional Requirements

## Goals and Scope
The system aims to support the investigation, monitoring, management, analysis, and reporting of public health outbreaks and bioterrorism events. Its scope includes collecting and analyzing data related to cases, contacts, exposures, specimens, and treatments to identify and contain health events. The system must be configurable for different event types and support data exchange with other public health systems.

## Stakeholders and User Stories
*   **Public Health Investigator:** Conducts case investigations and contact tracing to understand and contain the outbreak.
*   **Laboratory Technician:** Processes specimens and reports results to confirm diagnoses and guide the response.
*   **Epidemiologist:** Analyzes aggregated data and generates visualizations to identify trends and assess intervention effectiveness.
*   **System Administrator:** Configures the system for specific health events and manages deployment, security, and data synchronization.
*   **Public Health Official (Jurisdictional):** Oversees the response, makes decisions based on reported data, and coordinates with other agencies.
*   **Case/Contact Subject:** An individual (person, animal, or organization) who is investigated, monitored, or treated as part of the outbreak response.

**User Stories:**
1.  As a **Public Health Investigator**, I want to create and deploy electronic investigation questionnaires so that I can consistently collect case and exposure data in the field.
2.  As a **Laboratory Technician**, I want to link test results unambiguously to specific specimens and cases so that diagnoses are accurate and actionable.
3.  As an **Epidemiologist**, I want to generate maps and epi-curves from investigation data so that I can visualize the spread and impact of the health event.
4.  As a **System Administrator**, I want to configure new data fields and entity types so that the system can adapt to the unique needs of each new outbreak.
5.  As a **Public Health Official**, I want to receive aggregated reports on case counts and countermeasures administered so that I can track response progress and resource needs.
6.  As a **Public Health Investigator**, I want to trace and link exposure contacts across jurisdictions so that I can identify all at-risk populations.

## Key Processes
1.  **Event Initiation:** A new health event is identified and registered in the system (Trigger: Report of a potential outbreak).
2.  **Case Investigation:** Investigators collect data on confirmed, probable, and suspect cases using configurable electronic questionnaires.
3.  **Contact Tracing & Exposure Linking:** Identified cases are used to trace and investigate their contacts, establishing links between entities (people, places, animals).
4.  **Specimen Management & Lab Request:** Specimens are collected from subjects, logged with chain-of-custody data, and test requests are sent to laboratories.
5.  **Result Integration & Case Update:** Laboratory results are received, parsed, and linked back to specimens and cases, potentially updating case status.
6.  **Monitoring & Follow-up:** The status of cases and contacts under isolation, quarantine, or treatment is monitored and logged.
7.  **Analysis & Reporting:** Data is aggregated and analyzed to generate reports, visualizations, and share information with partner systems.

## Domain Data Elements
*   **Entity:** (Primary Key: Entity ID) Subject ID, Entity Type (e.g., Person, Animal, Location), Name/Description, Demographics/Location Details, Associated Event ID.
*   **Health Event:** (Primary Key: Event ID) Event Name/Description, Category (e.g., infectious, environmental), Start Date, Suspected Agent, Status (e.g., open, closed), Case Definition.
*   **Case/Investigation:** (Primary Key: Case ID) Entity ID, Event ID, Case Status (e.g., confirmed, probable), Diagnosis, Symptom Onset Date, Investigation Dates, Priority.
*   **Exposure Contact:** (Primary Key: Contact ID) Source Entity ID, Exposed Entity ID, Exposure Type, Exposure Dates/Times, Place of Exposure, Health Status.
*   **Specimen/Sample:** (Primary Key: Specimen ID) Subject ID, Collection Date/Time, Specimen Type, Suspected Agent, Collection Location, Chain of Custody Log.
*   **Laboratory Result:** (Primary Key: Result ID) Specimen ID, Test Date/Time, Test Type (e.g., LOINC), Result Value, Result Interpretation, Testing Laboratory.

## Non-Functional Requirements
1.  **Configurability:** The system must allow dynamic addition of data fields, entity types, and relationship types to suit specific health events.
2.  **Interoperability:** Must support bi-directional, standards-based data exchange (e.g., HL7, vocabulary standards) with laboratory, surveillance, and countermeasure systems.
3.  **Availability & Continuity:** Must have a plan for continuing operations during system downtime and support multiple deployment modes (client-server, disconnected, web).
4.  **Security & Privacy:** Must protect data from unauthorized access and corruption, and comply with privacy regulations for patient and entity information.
5.  **Data Integrity:** Must support structured data entry and validation to ensure data quality and standardization for exchange.
6.  **Scalability & Performance:** Must handle data collection, linking, and analysis for large-scale outbreaks across multiple jurisdictions.

## Milestones and External Dependencies
1.  Finalization and adoption of PHIN message implementation guides for laboratory orders/results and case reporting.
2.  Establishment of vocabulary standards (e.g., LOINC, SNOMED) and data dictionaries for key data elements.
3.  Development and validation of reusable, syndromic questionnaire libraries.
4.  Integration with external systems for Early Event Detection (EED), Surveillance, and Countermeasure/Response Administration.
5.  Definition of jurisdictional policies for data sharing, case reporting responsibilities, and cross-jurisdiction investigation protocols.

## Risks and Mitigation Strategies
1.  **Risk:** Inability to exchange data due to incompatible system configurations or non-standard data fields.
    *   **Mitigation:** Require use of standard vocabularies in exchanges and mandate clear data mapping interfaces for custom fields.
2.  **Risk:** System failure or unavailability during a critical outbreak response.
    *   **Mitigation:** Implement robust continuity of operations (COOP) plans and support reliable disconnected operation modes.
3.  **Risk:** Poor data quality from field collections hinders analysis and decision-making.
    *   **Mitigation:** Enforce structured data entry with validation and support barcode scanning for specimen data capture.
4.  **Risk:** Privacy breaches of sensitive health investigation data.
    *   **Mitigation:** Implement role-based access controls, audit logging, and adhere to strict privacy requirement guidelines.
5.  **Risk:** Complex configuration and setup delays response to a novel or fast-moving outbreak.
    *   **Mitigation:** Develop pre-configured "deployment packages" for known syndromes and establish rapid configuration protocols.

## Undecided Issues
1.  Specific technical protocols for data synchronization between disconnected clients and central servers.
2.  Detailed criteria for determining when monitoring shifts from contact tracing to a focused countermeasure administration effort.
3.  Exact file formats and technical specifications for importing/exporting data with common analytical tools (SAS, Excel, etc.).
4.  The complete set of "other descriptive details" to be captured for person entities beyond core demographics.
5.  Standardized methods for unambiguous identification of animal subjects in investigations.
6.  Finalized policies for communication workflows with external stakeholders like FEMA, media, and the public.
