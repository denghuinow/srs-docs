# Balanced Summary: Tarrant County IJIS - Manage Indigent Defense Services

## Goals and Scope
This system aims to manage the end-to-end process for providing court-appointed legal counsel to indigent defendants in Tarrant County, Texas. It seeks to automate and streamline the submission, processing, and administration of indigent defense services to ensure compliance with the Texas Fair Defense Act. The scope includes managing attorney appointments, defendant contacts, case notifications, attorney compensation, and comprehensive reporting.

## Stakeholders and User Stories
*   **Defendant**: An arrested/charged person who may request a court-appointed attorney if found indigent.
*   **Magistrate**: A judicial officer who informs defendants of their rights, determines indigency, and submits requests for counsel.
*   **Office of Attorney Appointments (OAA)**: The central office responsible for managing the entire indigent defense appointment process.
*   **Defense Attorney**: A qualified attorney appointed to represent an indigent defendant.
*   **Judge (County/District)**: Presides over cases, approves attorney payments, and manages attorney lists.
*   **Court Coordinator**: Manages court schedules and enters setting information into the case management system.
*   **District Attorney**: Represents the state, makes filing decisions on charges, and notifies the OAA.
*   **Auditor**: Manages county finances and processes approved payments to defense attorneys.
*   **Task Force on Indigent Defense (TFID)**: A state committee that establishes standards and requires compliance reporting.
*   **Court Case Management System**: An external system used by the criminal justice community to manage case information.

**User Stories:**
1.  As a Magistrate, I want to electronically submit a Request for Appointed Counsel so that the OAA can process it within mandated timeframes.
2.  As the Office of Attorney Appointments, I want to select the next qualified attorney from a rotation list so that defendants receive timely representation.
3.  As a Defense Attorney, I want to notify the OAA of my first contact with a defendant so that compliance with contact rules is recorded.
4.  As a District Attorney, I want to notify the OAA of my case filing decision so that appointed attorneys are informed of accepted/modified/rejected charges.
5.  As a Defense Attorney, I want to submit an itemized request for payment so that I can be compensated for services rendered.
6.  As the Task Force on Indigent Defense, I want to receive comprehensive reports from the OAA so that I can monitor compliance with state standards.

## Key Processes
1.  **Submit Request for Counsel** (Trigger: Defendant arrested and requests counsel). A magistrate determines indigency and submits the request to the OAA.
2.  **Process Request for Counsel** (Trigger: OAA receives a request). The OAA validates the request, checks for existing counsel, and appoints the next attorney from the appropriate rotation list.
3.  **Manage Defendant Contact** (Trigger: Attorney is appointed). The appointed attorney must contact the defendant and report the contact to the OAA.
4.  **Manage Case Filing Decision** (Trigger: District Attorney decides on prosecution). The OAA is notified of filed charges and updates the court system and defense attorney.
5.  **Manage Court Setting Notification** (Trigger: A court setting is scheduled). The OAA notifies relevant parties of hearings and records outcomes.
6.  **Manage Attorney Compensation** (Trigger: Attorney submits payment request). A judge approves the request, which is then processed for payment by the county auditor.
7.  **Manage Attorney List** (Trigger: Attorney applies to list or judge orders change). The OAA manages the qualification and rotation lists for appointed attorneys.

## Domain Data Elements
*   **Defendant**: (Key: Defendant ID). Fields: Name, Financial Affidavit Status, Indigency Status, Custody Status.
*   **Request for Appointed Counsel**: (Key: Request ID). Fields: Submission Date/Time, Magistrate ID, Defendant ID, Determination (Indigent/Not).
*   **Defense Attorney**: (Key: Attorney ID). Fields: Name, Qualification Level, List Status (Active/Suspended), Rotation List Assignment.
*   **Appointment**: (Key: Appointment ID). Fields: Attorney ID, Defendant ID, Charge/Offense Type, Appointment Date, First Contact Date.
*   **Case/Charge**: (Key: Case/Charge ID). Fields: Offense Type, Filing Status (Accepted/Rejected/Modified), Disposition.
*   **Payment Request**: (Key: Payment Request ID). Fields: Attorney ID, Services Itemized, Hours Billed, Judge Approval Status, Payment Amount.

## Non-Functional Requirements
1.  **Legal Compliance**: Must adhere to Texas Code of Criminal Procedure (CCP) articles and Texas Fair Defense Act standards.
2.  **Integration**: Must interface with external Court Case Management and District Attorney case filing systems.
3.  **Electronic Records Management**: Must follow Texas guidelines for managing electronic transactions and signed records.
4.  **Timeliness**: Processes must support mandated timeframes (e.g., appointment within one working day of request receipt).
5.  **Reporting**: Must generate extensive, configurable reports for the Task Force on Indigent Defense as per Texas Government Code.
6.  **Auditability**: Must maintain a complete record of all actions for compliance auditing.

## Milestones and External Dependencies
1.  Finalize business requirements through community workshops.
2.  Secure approval from the Tarrant County Commissioners Court.
3.  Develop integration interfaces with the external Court Case Management System.
4.  Develop integration with the District Attorney's Electronic Case Filing System (ECFS).
5.  Coordinate with the County Auditor for integration with the financial system (SAP).

## Risks and Mitigation Strategies
1.  **Risk**: Complex integration with legacy justice systems may cause delays.
    *   **Mitigation**: Early engagement with system custodians and phased integration planning.
2.  **Risk**: Resistance to new electronic processes from judicial staff or attorneys.
    *   **Mitigation**: Comprehensive training programs and phased rollout with super-user support.
3.  **Risk**: Failing to meet stringent state-mandated timelines for appointments and reporting.
    *   **Mitigation**: Build robust workflow automation with escalation alerts for overdue tasks.
4.  **Risk**: Legal challenges regarding the electronic determination and signing of indigency affidavits.
    *   **Mitigation**: Ensure system design strictly follows the Texas Uniform Electronic Transactions Act (UETA).
5.  **Risk**: Inaccurate data in external systems (e.g., Court Case Management) leading to incorrect appointments.
    *   **Mitigation**: Implement validation checks and clear error-handling procedures for data discrepancies.
